How Elections Work

The Election Journey

From announcement to results — follow every step of the democratic process.

1
📢

Election Announcement

Pre-Election ▼

The Election Commission announces dates and sets the process in motion.

The Election Commission of India announces the election schedule, including nomination deadlines, polling dates, and counting day. The Model Code of Conduct comes into effect immediately.

  • Schedule notification by Election Commission
  • Model Code of Conduct enforced
  • Voter rolls finalized
2
📝

Nomination Filing

Pre-Election ▼

Candidates file nominations and undergo scrutiny.

Aspiring candidates submit their nomination papers along with a security deposit. These are scrutinized by the Returning Officer. Candidates may withdraw within a specified period.

  • Filing of nomination papers
  • Scrutiny by Returning Officer
  • Withdrawal period for candidates
  • Final list of contesting candidates
3
📣

Election Campaigning

Campaign ▼

Parties and candidates reach out to voters.

Political parties and candidates campaign through rallies, advertisements, door-to-door outreach, and social media. Campaigning must stop 48 hours before polling — the "silence period."

  • Public rallies and roadshows
  • Media advertisements and debates
  • Social media campaigns
  • Campaigning ends 48 hours before polls
4
🗳️

Polling Day

Voting ▼

Citizens exercise their right to vote using EVMs.

Voters go to their assigned polling booths, verify identity, and cast votes using Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs) with VVPAT verification. The process is monitored by election officials and observers.

  • Voter identity verification at polling booth
  • Voting via EVM with VVPAT slip
  • Indelible ink applied to prevent fraud
  • NOTA option available for all voters
5
📊

Counting & Results

Post-Election ▼

Votes are counted and winners are declared.

On counting day, EVM votes are tallied round by round. VVPAT slips from randomly selected booths are cross-verified. Results are announced constituency-wise and the party/alliance with majority forms the government.

  • Round-by-round EVM counting
  • VVPAT verification of random booths
  • Results declared by Returning Officer
  • Government formation process begins

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

The minimum age to vote in India is 18 years. Any citizen who has attained the age of 18 on or before the qualifying date (1st January of the year of revision of the electoral roll) is eligible to register as a voter.
You can apply for a Voter ID (EPIC) online through the National Voters' Service Portal (NVSP) at nvsp.in or the Voter Helpline App. Fill Form 6 with your details, upload proof of age, address, and photograph.
NOTA (None of the Above) allows voters to officially reject all candidates. Introduced by the Supreme Court in 2013, it's the last button on the EVM. Even if NOTA gets the highest votes, the leading candidate still wins.
The Model Code of Conduct (MCC) is a set of guidelines by the Election Commission to ensure free and fair elections. It takes effect from the date of election announcement until results are declared.
Currently, you must vote at your assigned polling station. If you've moved, apply to transfer your registration. Service voters, overseas electors, and election duty personnel can use postal ballots.
An EVM is a portable electronic device with no network connectivity. Manufactured by BEL and ECIL with strict security. VVPAT adds transparency by printing a paper slip of each vote for verification.
